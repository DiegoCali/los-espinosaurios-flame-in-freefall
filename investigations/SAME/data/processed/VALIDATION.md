## Material audit: the 10 unlabeled tests

**Question.** Are the tests without a material label calibration or purge (blank) runs, and can their material be recovered?

**Cross-check of sources.** The three documents were searched for these run numbers. `Sample descriptions tables.docx` and `thermal precipitator map updated.xlsx` together cover 36 of the 47 telemetry tests; none of the ten appears in either (no overlap found), so no thermal-precipitator grid was analysed for them. `ISS Coagulation.doc` has no run identifiers (three lampwick/Teflon examples with start/end concentrations only). The GMT timestamps in the CSV files cannot be matched to the documents, whose dates are TEM viewing dates, not run times.

**Not blank runs.** Calibration or purge periods in the telemetry are the `TestNumber = 0` records between tests, which are excluded. All ten are numbered tests with heating on for 52–54 s, plume temperature up to 61–115 °C and one or two pushes. Nine produced a measurable detector response; test 90 produced almost none (DustTrak peak 0.12 mg/m³), a heated test with negligible smoke rather than a blank.

**Material recovered from the telemetry itself.** The sample is wound on its heater wire; the wire's cold resistance and the hot/cold ratio Rt/R0 depend on the sample and its temperature regime. Telemetry Rt/R0 equals NASA's docx value within 0.02 on every labelled test. A 1-nearest-neighbour classifier on (cold R, Rt/R0) agrees with the known material for 34 of 36 labelled tests (leave-one-out); the two misses are test 73 (the Kapton/silicone boundary) and test 92 (below).

**Reading.** Seven tests (59, 65, 66, 86, 91, 99, 103) fall in the silicone class and three (61, 90, 102) in the Kapton class. Silicone and Kapton are the closest pair, so test 91 (margin below 2.5) is the least certain. DBP-on-wick samples, which NASA lists among the six materials, could share the silicone cluster; they cannot be separated here. Test 59 is consistent with the analyst's file name `silcon_run59`, and tests 99 and 103 would be the only silicone no-flow runs (the paper notes silicone and DBP give no visible TEM particles, which fits their absence from the grid map). **These are inferences, not NASA labels.** They are exported as `material_inferred` and were not used to build the frozen model datasets.

## Test 92: a label that the data contradict

Test 92 was assigned lampwick because a labelled MSG video (`1 Lampwick`) started 10 s after the test record. Its heater fingerprint (0.645 Ω, Rt/R0 1.368) matches Kapton (≈0.64 Ω, 1.37–1.47), not lampwick (≈0.76 Ω, 1.24–1.28), and its weak response (DustTrak 5.3 mg/m³) is Kapton-like. The video label is probably unrelated to this test. The frozen run keeps the original label so that the archived AutoResearch evaluation is not rewritten; the effect is small:

| Scenario | Pushes | Detection Brier ratio | ISS accuracy | Ionization accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| As run (92 = lampwick) | 65 | 0.491 | 88% | 89% |
| 92 relabelled by fingerprint | 65 | 0.511 | 86% | 89% |
| Also add the 10 inferred tests | 84 | 0.499 | 89% | 88% |

The model is frozen from attempt 11; only the evaluation data change. All scenarios stay far below 1.0 (the mean-prevalence baseline), so the conclusions do not depend on these labels. A new study should re-run the search with the audited labels under a new run directory.
